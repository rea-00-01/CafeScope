
CafeScope: Jaipur Cafe Market Analysis

1. Executive Summary

CafeScope analyzes the Jaipur cafe market using data on pricing, ratings, review volumes, locations, cuisines, and business offerings across 552 cafes. Of the 552 cafes, 527 have a classified price segment. Mid-range cafes account for 39.28% of this classified group, while budget cafes account for 37.38%. The average dining rating is 3.51, and the average listed spend for two is ₹415.65. Initial neighborhood analysis identifies C Scheme, Lal Kothi, and Durgapura as areas worth investigating because they combine meaningful cafe concentrations with promising rating and review-volume indicators. These findings support exploring a value-conscious, quality-focused cafe concept, subject to further competitive and location analysis.

Premium cafes have the highest average dining rating in this dataset, at 3.92, but they also have the highest average listed cost for two. Budget cafes have the lowest average listed cost and an average rating of 3.35.

These comparisons suggest a potential trade-off between affordability and reported customer ratings. They do not establish that higher prices cause higher ratings. Differences in cafe type, location, sample size, and customer expectations may also contribute.

Twenty-five cafes do not have a classified price segment and should be investigated separately.

3. Neighborhood Analysis

Initial results show that Malviya Nagar, Mansarovar, C Scheme, and Vaishali Nagar have some of the largest cafe concentrations in the dataset.

C Scheme has 46 cafes, an average dining rating of 3.82, an average listed cost for two of ₹540.79, and approximately 193 dining reviews per cafe.

Lal Kothi has 19 cafes, an average rating of 3.66, an average listed cost for two of ₹494.74, and approximately 223 dining reviews per cafe.

Durgapura has 18 cafes, an average rating of 3.62, an average listed cost for two of ₹459.38, and approximately 191 dining reviews per cafe.

These areas are candidates for further evaluation, not confirmed optimal launch locations. The final neighborhood comparison should consider cafe concentration, average ratings, review volume, price levels, and the number of observations available for each area.

The final top-10 neighborhood table will be added after validation.

5. Business Recommendation

The initial findings support investigating a budget-to-mid-range cafe concept that balances accessible pricing with a strong customer experience.

C Scheme, Lal Kothi, and Durgapura are promising candidates for further investigation, but a final location should not be selected based on ratings or review counts alone.

Before recommending a specific neighborhood, the analysis should compare:

* Number of existing cafes.
* Average dining rating.
* Average and total dining-review volume.
* Average listed cost for two.
* Competitive positioning of established cafes.

Pricing should be benchmarked against comparable cafes in the selected neighborhood rather than derived directly from the citywide average.

The available data does not establish rent, operating costs, customer footfall, sales, profitability, or unmet demand. Those factors would require additional research before making an investment decision.

6. Methodology

The project uses a Jaipur cafe dataset containing business information, location, listed prices, ratings, review counts, cuisines, and operational attributes.

The analytical workflow includes:

1. Data audit.
2. Data cleaning.
3. Exploratory data analysis.
4. Feature engineering.
5. Loading the processed data into MySQL.
6. SQL-based market and competitor analysis.

Derived variables support analysis of cafe eligibility, price segments, cuisine counts, and review volumes.

7. Limitations

* The dataset represents the cafes captured by the source, not necessarily every cafe in Jaipur.
* Missing prices and ratings may affect comparisons.
* Review counts are indicators of customer engagement, not direct business-performance measures.
* Neighborhood averages may be unstable when based on few cafes.
* The current dataset does not provide the review text needed for review-level sentiment analysis.
* The current dataset does not provide the coordinates needed for reliable geospatial analysis.
* A single dataset snapshot cannot establish historical growth or seasonal patterns.

8. Conclusion

CafeScope demonstrates how data cleaning, feature engineering, MySQL, and business-oriented SQL analysis can be combined to investigate a city’s cafe market.

The initial findings indicate that budget and mid-range cafes form a substantial portion of the classified dataset, while several neighborhoods and established cafes offer useful subjects for deeper competitive analysis.

The next stage is to validate the final neighborhood and cafe benchmark tables, then translate the strongest findings into a concise dashboard and portfolio-ready presentation.
